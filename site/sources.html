Sources & methodology

What the model is

A proprietary estimate of how much crude oil, condensate and petroleum products are moving through the Strait of Hormuz on a given day, expressed in million barrels per day, built only from inputs we are legally permitted to republish.

What is in it today

One cleared dataset, and we say so rather than implying a wider net: the U.S. Energy Information Administration’s Global Energy Security Data, Table 4, released 12 August 2026, covering quarters through April–June 2026. It is a work of the U.S. federal government and is in the public domain. EIA’s volumes are based on Vortexa tanker tracking data with additional EIA analysis; we republish EIA’s published analysis and do not hold or license Vortexa’s underlying data.

EIA notes that since the end of February 2026, AIS signal data for ships transiting the strait has become especially unreliable, and that 2026 Hormuz volumes are being revised frequently as better information arrives. We treat the anchor as provisional for exactly that reason.

What is deliberately not in it

Several daily trackers exist. We have checked their terms and rejected them: IMF PortWatch and the WTO/AXSMarine Hormuz tracker are both closed to redistribution. We do not use an uncleared source “just to calibrate” — if it moves the number, it needs a licence.

How the estimate is built

The latest published quarterly average is the anchor. With no cleared higher-frequency input, we carry it forward unchanged rather than inventing a shape inside the quarter that we cannot observe — a smoother curve would be false precision, not better information.

The range is not a guess about a guess. We back-tested the same method against the published record: predicting each quarter from the one before was accurate to within 1.4% while flows were steady, then overshot by 45% and by 204% as flows collapsed through 2026. A range wide enough to have contained that collapse runs from about 70% below the anchor to about 40% above it — today, 1.5 to 6.9 million barrels per day. A mechanical test decides which regime we are in: anything beyond ±10% quarter-on-quarter is “disrupted”, and the range widens accordingly. Every time a new quarter publishes, the anchor resets, the previous estimate is scored against the actual, and that error joins the record the range is derived from.

Where this is going

The method is the product, and it is not finished. Expanding the set of inputs — additional public datasets, official reports, credible reporting — is standing, continuous work. Each addition has to clear the same bar: we must be legally permitted to use and republish it. When the set changes, this page changes with it.

The full working method, including the back-test and every source checked and rejected on licensing grounds, is in the public project repository.
