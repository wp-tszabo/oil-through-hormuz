How we get to a number

Nobody publishes a daily figure for the Strait of Hormuz that is free to republish. The public record arrives a quarter at a time, months after the fact. The trackers that watch the strait day by day are locked behind commercial licences.

So we build our own. Our model takes the public evidence we can legally stand behind, calibrates against the quarterly record, moves with monthly Gulf oil production data, and carries the result forward to the most recent completed day (UTC) — with a range that reflects how much we genuinely know rather than how confident we would like to sound. Right now that range is wide: flows fell from 21.6 to 4.9 million barrels per day in three quarters, and our model knows it is estimating through a disruption. When flows settle, the range closes to around ±3%.

This is our estimate, not an official measurement — and that is the product. The method is ours, it is published in full, and it is scored against every new figure that lands. Widening what feeds it — more public data, official reports, credible reporting — is continuous work, and the sources and method page always says exactly what is in the model today.
