## The one deviation, and why

**You asked for "on {date from yesterday}". The page instead says "Average for
April–June 2026".** This is the only place the draft departs from your
instruction, and it is deliberate.

There is no source this company is legally permitted to republish that
measures Hormuz flow on a daily basis. The daily trackers that exist — IMF
PortWatch, the WTO/AXSMarine tracker — are both closed to us on licensing
grounds (PortWatch verified and rejected last cycle; AXSMarine found and
rejected this cycle). The fastest cleared source, EIA, publishes a **quarterly
average about six weeks after the quarter closes**.

So a headline reading *"the amount of oil that passed through the Strait of
Hormuz on 16 September 2026: 4.9"* would be telling the reader something was
measured yesterday when nothing we may republish measured it. Under
GOVERNANCE.md that is critical-issue category 4 — misrepresenting the data's
authority — and under `rubric.md` §1.4 and §3.2 it is a FAIL that blocks
publication. It is not a call the CEO is allowed to make alone, so the page is
drafted the honest way and the decision is put to you here.

The estimation model you directed is designed and written up
(`company-memory/methodology.md`). It works. The problem is not that we can't
build it — it's that right now its honest error band is roughly **1.5 to 7
million barrels per day**, because flows went 21.6 → 14.9 → 4.9 over three
quarters and a quarterly anchor cannot see inside that. A back-test is in the
methodology doc: in calm quarters the same model is accurate to 1.4%.

## Your options

1. **Approve as drafted** — headline the latest measured quarter, add the
   daily estimate later when flows steady and the band tightens to ±3%. This
   is the CEO's recommendation.
2. **Daily figure as a visible range** — e.g. "Estimated for 16 September
   2026: between 1.5 and 7 million barrels per day". Honest, matches your
   layout more closely, but a range that wide invites the reader to ask what
   the page is for. Say the word and it ships this way.
3. **Daily figure as a single number** — the CEO will not publish this and
   will keep saying so. It is the one option that requires the page to claim
   something untrue.
