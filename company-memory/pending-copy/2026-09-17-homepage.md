- **No figures appear anywhere in this draft, deliberately.** The CEO cannot
  currently re-fetch any source to verify a number (see critical issue #1), and
  rubric §1 forbids publishing a figure that can't be traced and spot-checked.
  Every place a live number will eventually sit is marked `[FIGURE: …]` and is
  *not* part of what you're approving.
- What you *are* approving is the prose: the headline, the positioning, and
  above all the honesty framing — how the site describes where its numbers come
  from and how much to trust them.

## Draft C — "How this works" (methodology blurb)

> **How this works**
>
> There is no meter on the Strait of Hormuz. Nobody counts barrels as they go
> past. Every public figure for how much oil moves through it — including the
> ones on this page — is an estimate, built either from national export
> statistics or from satellite tracking of the ships themselves.
>
> This site takes one public source, shows you what it says, and tells you
> when it was published and when we fetched it. We don't blend sources into a
> single number of our own, and we don't fill gaps with guesses. If the source
> hasn't updated, the page says so instead of showing you a stale number as if
> it were current.
>
> Two limits worth knowing about. First, cadence: the most reliable public
> sources publish on a monthly or quarterly rhythm, so a figure here can
> legitimately be weeks old. Second, ship-tracking data depends on vessels
> broadcasting their position, and in this particular waterway some of them
> don't — GPS jamming, spoofed signals, and vessels switching transponders off
> are all documented problems. Estimates built on that data carry real
> uncertainty, and tighten only after the fact.
>
> We'd rather show you a number with its caveats attached than a confident one
> that quietly isn't.

## Why it's written this way (CEO rationale)

- Rubric §3 forbids implying official authority. Drafts C, D and E each say
  plainly that this is a derived estimate from someone else's data, and D
  contains an explicit non-affiliation line.
- Rubric §1 requires staleness be surfaced rather than hidden. That is designed
  into the figure block (B) and stated as a promise in C, so the site can't
  quietly drift into showing an old number as current.
- Draft C names the AIS jamming/spoofing/dark-vessel problem directly. It is a
  documented condition in this waterway and it is the single most likely way
  this site would be confidently wrong. Burying it would be the dishonest
  choice.
- All prose is original. No sentence is lifted from EIA, the IMF, or any other
  source; nothing is quoted, so no quotation attribution is required.
