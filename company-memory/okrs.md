### Phase 1 evaluation — running notes (for the graduation decision)

GOVERNANCE.md asks three questions before Phase 2 is proposed. Partial answers so far, to be finalised at week close.

**0. A fourth finding that belongs above the three, because it conditions all of them: the company has never once run as designed.** The Agent tool has been unavailable for **seven consecutive cycles** — every cycle of the company's existence. Research and Build have received nothing. Every consequential call — clearing EIA, rejecting PortWatch, WTO and JODI on licence, back-testing the model, deciding a frozen date warranted a live fix without owner review, constructing GPCI and judging that it contradicts our own published figure — was made *and reviewed* by the same agent. The designed control was a specialist producing work and a CEO reviewing it; what actually runs is one agent marking its own homework, with the rubric and the owner checkpoints as the only real external checks. Those two have held up well, which is the good news. But any Phase 2 proposal must be read knowing that the three answers below were written by the same process they are assessing.

1. **Did the research approach hold up?** Mixed, and honestly: the guilty-until-checked licence rule has now worked **three** times (PortWatch, WTO/AXSMarine, and JODI-Oil on 2026-09-18 — all rejected before any code depended on them), and the third was the real test, because JODI was the single most valuable thing the standing mandate has turned up and it was refused on a first-hand read of its terms. The *search* was weak for two cycles — a 14-month staleness claim that was simply wrong because nobody asked whether EIA had a newer product. That lesson has since paid for itself twice: re-applying it on 2026-09-18 found that the EIA supplement has ten tables, not one, yielding a second cleared input for free. The rule caught legal risk; curiosity had to be installed deliberately.
2. **Did the checkpoints fire when they should have?** Yes, and now four times for real: the copy checkpoint carried a disagreement with the owner's instruction upward instead of resolving it silently; the rubric caught a factual error pre-publish; and on 2026-09-18 the **category-1 stale-data interrupt fired exactly as designed on a failure a previous cycle had predicted in writing** — the page was checked first, the defect was found, the issue was raised before any other work, and the durable fix was held for approval rather than self-merged. Two fired late or imperfectly: broken HTTPS sat in the backlog as routine for a day before being re-classified critical, and the stale date was allowed to *become* live rather than being pre-empted by shipping the automation alongside the dated headline. The honest lesson: this company is good at noticing failures and slow at removing their causes.
3. **Is free-tier data good enough?** **Better than believed twice over, and the answer is still moving in the free tier's favour.** On 2026-09-19 the free tier produced a *monthly* cleared series with history two months fresher than the quarterly anchor, at zero new licence surface — from the publisher we had already cleared, simply by asking whether it had a higher-cadence product. That is the second time in three cycles that the binding constraint turned out to be **our own incuriosity rather than the free tier's limits**, which is a direct argument *against* proposing paid data yet: we have not finished mining what is free. The daily gap remains genuinely unclosable free — the constraint there is licensing, not cost. *(Prior assessment retained below.)* Better than believed, and still not sufficient for the product as specified. Quarterly at ~11 weeks is genuinely publishable; daily is unavailable at any free price because the constraint is licensing, not cost. **A paid feed would close the daily gap — which is exactly why the case for it must wait until the owner has decided whether the honest quarterly product is the product.** Not proposing graduation.
