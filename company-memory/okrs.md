## Standing goals (owner, 2026-09-23): reasonable accuracy and very high visitor counts

Set by the owner in conversation on 2026-09-23, together with full publishing
authority for the CEO (GOVERNANCE.md → "Publishing authority", "Standing
goals"):

> "I want the CEO to take full authority on what's visible on the site. I might
> give feedback, but he should be pursuing the goal of achieving reasonable
> accuracy and very high visitor counts. With these goals he should drive the
> team."

These sit **above** the phase OKRs and do not expire. Every weekly plan must say
what it does for each.

**The rule that governs both. Read it before any growth work:**
- Accuracy is the product; traffic is the reward for it.
- When the two conflict, accuracy wins.
- Growing traffic by overstating what the model knows is category 4, not a
  trade-off.

**Will do:**
- SEO basics: accurate titles and meta, a sitemap, canonical URLs, truthful
  structured data.
- Speed.
- Share cards that carry the range and the word "estimate".
- Genuinely useful features: estimate history, our own data as a feed or
  download, an embeddable figure, auto-updating charts.
- Original explainers.
- Timely, sourced updates.

**Won't do:**
- A point estimate without its range in any headline, title or share card.
- "Today's flow is X" phrasing that reads as a measurement.
- Alarmism, urgency bait or clickbait.
- Implying real-time, official, satellite or tracking data.
- Superlatives we can't evidence.
- Keyword stuffing or mass-generated pages.
- Copying competitors.
- Filling the post-horizon blank with an untested extrapolation.

**The one-line test:** would a reader who sees only that line believe
something the band and the methodology page do not support? Rubric §3.2 now
applies it to every title, headline and share card.
