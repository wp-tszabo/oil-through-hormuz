## Standing goals (owner, 2026-09-23): reasonable accuracy and very high visitor counts

Set by the owner in conversation on 2026-09-23, together with full publishing
authority for the CEO (GOVERNANCE.md → "Publishing authority", "Standing
goals"):

> "I want the CEO to take full authority on what's visible on the site. I might
> give feedback, but he should be pursuing the goal of achieving reasonable
> accuracy and very high visitor counts. With these goals he should drive the
> team."

These sit **above** the phase OKRs and do not expire. Every weekly plan must say
what it does for each.

**The rule that governs both. Read it before any growth work:**
- Accuracy is the product; traffic is the reward for it.
- When the two conflict, accuracy wins.
- Growing traffic by overstating what the model knows is category 4, not a
  trade-off.

**Will do:**
- SEO basics: accurate titles and meta, a sitemap, canonical URLs, truthful
  structured data.
- Speed.
- Share cards that carry the range and the word "estimate".
- Genuinely useful features: estimate history, our own data as a feed or
  download, an embeddable figure, auto-updating charts.
- Original explainers.
- Timely, sourced updates.

**Won't do:**
- A point estimate without its range in any headline, title or share card.
- "Today's flow is X" phrasing that reads as a measurement.
- Alarmism, urgency bait or clickbait.
- Implying real-time, official, satellite or tracking data.
- Superlatives we can't evidence.
- Keyword stuffing or mass-generated pages.
- Copying competitors.
- Filling the post-horizon blank with an untested extrapolation.

**The one-line test:** would a reader who sees only that line believe
something the band and the methodology page do not support? Rubric §3.2 now
applies it to every title, headline and share card.

### G3 operating rule (owner directive, 2026-10-29)

The CEO checks GoatCounter every cycle, records the reporting period and total
hits in `metrics.md`, and compares the result with the prior period. If the
analytics data cannot be read, record traffic as unmeasured. Each weekly plan
must name at least one specific, in-scope action to improve organic discovery
or return visits, with its outcome reviewed in the next cycle. Do not invent a
numeric growth target while traffic is too sparse to distinguish discovery from
testing; this supersedes the earlier G3 suggestion to use week-on-week growth
until a reliable baseline exists. This does not authorize outreach or posting in
the owner's name, spending beyond the approved ceiling, or monetization before
Phase 2 approval. Accuracy remains ahead of traffic.

### KR6 operating rule (owner directive, 2026-10-29)

The CEO delegates Research a prediction-focused KR6 brief every cycle. Research
must look for new or updated data and information relevant to Hormuz flows and
test what the evidence implies for the current estimator, back-test, uncertainty,
and failure modes. A source list alone is not a complete KR6 response: provide a
reproducible analysis when feasible, recommend a supported model improvement, or
record the concrete checks and evidence for no change. The CEO reviews the work
and decides methodology. The existing licence, accuracy, and tested-range rules
remain binding.

### KR6 prediction mandate (owner directive, 2026-11-16)

Supersedes the framing above where they conflict. Research's KR6 work is to
**establish a baseline and build a prediction model** of current Strait of
Hormuz traffic from all available information. That includes news, YouTube,
X posts, maritime advisories and official statistics, under the tier-1/tier-2
rules in GOVERNANCE.md → "Prediction mandate and information sources".
Official figures (EIA and others) are ground truth for **validating and
improving** the prediction, not events to wait for. Any publicly reachable
dataset, whatever its licence (e.g. IMF PortWatch), may be used internally
as tier-3 validation data, never published. Each cycle reports the
current prediction and band, signals logged in `signal-log.md`, and the
latest measured validation error. Success is a prediction whose measured
error against official figures shrinks over time.
